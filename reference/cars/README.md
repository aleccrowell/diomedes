# Car reference tables

Chassis and engine identities for the race model's car walks
([#95](https://github.com/aleccrowell/diomedes/issues/95)): each car-season's effect combines a
**chassis** part (a random walk over the seasons of the works that built the car), an **engine**
part (a random walk over the seasons of the works that built the engine) and a residual per
car-season. Rows marked "uncertain" say why in `notes`; every hand-made row cites its sources.

## `entries.csv`: each race entry's chassis constructor and engine maker (generated)

Jolpica has no engine, and its constructor keys mix chassis-engine pairs (`cooper-climax`,
1950s–60s) with chassis names. `scripts/cars/match_f1db.jl` matches each Jolpica race entry
1950–2025 to the [F1DB](https://github.com/f1db/f1db) open database (release v2026.17.0), which
records the chassis constructor and engine maker of every entry. Within each race, a Jolpica row
matches the F1DB entries whose driver surname is in its name, with ties broken by first name,
laps and constructor. 25,383 of 25,468 rows match. The other 85 are entries that never started (0
laps, not in F1DB's race results) and one mangled name (1976 Dutch GP, "Boy Lunger"); the model
gives an unmatched row its car-season's most common chassis and engine.

Where Jolpica's key and F1DB's constructor differ, F1DB is the more precise chassis maker: e.g.
Larrousse's Lola and Venturi chassis, the 1952 BMW-engined specials (Heck, Balsa, …) Jolpica files
as `bmw`, the Lancias entered by Ferrari in 1955.

## `works.csv`: the works that built each constructor's cars

`works_id` per F1DB constructor and year range. A **works** is the organisation that builds the
car. The rule: **same works if the factory and core design staff carried over**. A rename or a
sponsor change keeps the works (Toro Rosso → AlphaTauri → RB, Racing Point → Aston Martin);
a change of owner keeps it too, and is recorded in `ownership_changes.csv`. Chains follow F1DB's
constructor chronology, then three kinds of judgement:

- **One name, several works:** `renault` (Viry 1977–85; Enstone from 2002), `mercedes` (Stuttgart
  1954–55; Brackley from 2010), `honda` (1964–68; Brackley 2006–08), `alfa-romeo` (1950–51;
  1979–85; Hinwil 2019–23), `aston-martin` (1959–60; Silverstone from 2021).
- **Breaks where F1DB chains:** BAR from Tyrrell (new Brackley factory and design team) and
  Andrea Moda from Coloni (new team, Simtek-designed cars).
- **Uncertain links:** Prost as Ligier (same team, moved to Guyancourt in 1998), March 1981–82
  and 1987–89 as March 1970–77, Theodore 1983 as Ensign (the merged team raced the Ensign N183).

Customer cars share their maker's works (privateer Lotuses, Coopers, Lolas, Marches); the
per-car-season residual absorbs works-versus-customer differences. Generated by
`scripts/cars/write_works.py` (158 rows, 118 works, 6 uncertain).

## `ownership_changes.csv`: changes of a works' owner

`season` is the **first season raced entirely under the new owner** (Renault bought Benetton in
March 2000, so 2001). The chassis walk takes a larger step there (a learned multiplier). 49 rows,
10 uncertain. All rows were source-checked against Wikipedia and grandprix.com (and Motor Sport,
forix where available); the check corrected Arrows/Footwork (1991 → 1990), Ligier/de Rouvre
(1992 → 1993) and Ligier/Briatore (1994 → 1995, sources conflict) and added eleven changes
(Brabham 1971; Arrows 1996, 1999; Sauber 1995, 2002; Minardi 1994, 1997, 2009; Larrousse 1993;
AGS 1990). Changes before a team's first race (HRT/Carabante 2010) and those inside a season a
team didn't finish (Shadow/Yip 1980) need no row. Wolf 1977 is a change of control rather than
of shares.

## `engine_builders.csv`: the works behind each engine name

`builder_id` per F1DB engine maker and year range; an engine walk follows the builder.
Rebadged engines are keyed by their builder: TAG = Porsche; TAG Heuer and Toro Rosso 2017 =
Renault; Acer and Petronas = Ferrari; Megatron = BMW; Asiatech = Peugeot; European 2001 and
Fondmetal 2000 = Cosworth; Ford-badged Cosworths (DFV, HB, Zetec-R, CR) = Cosworth; RBPT and
Honda RBPT = Honda; BWT Mercedes = Mercedes. Ilmor (1991–94) and Sauber 1993 are the Brixworth
works that built Mercedes' engines from 1994, so they share Mercedes' walk; Mercedes' 1954–55
engine is a separate works, as are BMW's 1952–54 privateer engines. Uncertain: Mecachrome,
Playlife and Supertec as the Renault lineage, Arrows 1998–99 as Hart, Osella 1988 as Alfa
Romeo, and Mugen as its own works (never Honda-owned, but its engines came from Honda's V10s).
Generated by `scripts/cars/write_engines.py` (72 rows, 52 builders, 6 uncertain).

## `pace_regulations.csv`: major regulation changes for pace

Each walk takes a larger step (a learned multiplier) across a regulation change tagged for it:
**chassis**, **engine**, or both. Only **major** changes are listed, since minor ones would
dilute the multiplier. Source: Wikipedia's *History of Formula One regulations* and season
articles.

| tag | seasons |
|---|---|
| both | 1952 (World Championship to Formula Two rules), 1954, 1961, 1966 (new formulas) |
| chassis | 1983 (flat bottoms), 1994 (electronic aids ban, post-Imola), 1998 (narrow track, grooved tyres), 2009, 2017, 2022 |
| engine | 1984 (fuel limit), 1986 (turbos only), 1987, 1988 (boost limits), 1989 (turbo ban), 1995 (3.0 L), 2006 (V8), 2014 (hybrids) |

Left out as not major: 1958 (pump fuel), 1981–82 (skirt and ride-height rules), 1991, 1993,
2000 (V10 mandate, when every engine already was one), 2005, 2010–13 aero and tyre tweaks, 2019.
