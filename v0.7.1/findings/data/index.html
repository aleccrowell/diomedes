Data downloads

Every chart on these pages is drawn from the CSV files below: posterior summaries exported from named fits by scripts/export_site.jl. Each directory has a manifest.json recording which fit the tables came from, its data, its convergence, and when and from which commit they were exported. Effects in % are on the log scale 100·log(T / T_ref); negative is faster. Intervals are 90% posterior intervals (q05, q95). sd is the posterior standard deviation.

Race-model files

F1 1950–2026 (Jolpica API; 2026 through the 17 races run so far), best pace + loss model. The manifest also records the modes the chains settled in, convergence by group, and how far the effects differ between modes. manifest.json

FileOne row perColumns
drivers.csvdriver id, name; rows: results used; first_season, last_season; mean, sd, q05, q95: driver effect, % of race time in average-season units (κ = 1), relative to the average driver; slope_mean, slope_sd: career slope, % per decade of age, relative to the average driver's trajectory; incident_mean, incident_q05, incident_q95: incident proneness, offset on the log-odds of an incident, relative to the average driver; pace_part_mean: the driver effect; incident_part_mean, incident_part_q05, incident_part_q95: race time the driver's incident proneness adds (negative = saves) in a reference race with the average incident rate and loss size; overall_mean, overall_q05, overall_q95: the two together; all three % of race time in average-season units (see Speed and incident proneness)
cars.csvcar-season id (constructor_season), constructor, season, rows; mean, sd, q05, q95: car effect, % of race time relative to the other cars in the same season; dev_mean, dev_sd: in-season development trend, % from the season's first round to its last, relative to the field; speed_*, rough_*, temp_* (mean, sd): circuit slopes, % of race time per standard deviation of the race's average speed, road-surface roughness and air temperature, relative to the car's own races and to its field (positive = relatively slower); chassis_mean, engine_mean, works_mean, residual_mean: the car effect's mean split into its chassis-works walk, engine-builder walk, works-team term and residual parts, each centred within season (they add up to mean)
walks.csvwalk × season family (chassis or engine), walk (the works or engine builder, as in the car reference tables), season; mean, q05, q95: the walk's state, % of race time in average-season units, centred within season (negative = faster); own_change, reg_change: the step into this season crosses a change of ownership or a major regulation change
ranks.csvdriver with ≥ 20 results id, name, rows; p_top1, p_top3, p_top10: share of the posterior draws in which the driver ranks fastest, in the top 3, in the top 10 among these drivers; rank_median, rank_q05, rank_q95
driver_seasons.csvdriver × car-season raced driver, name, season, car, constructor, rows; driver_*: driver part, κ_season × (driver effect + career terms averaged over that season's results); car_*: car part (car-season effect); total_*: their sum. All in that season's % units (mean, q05, q95), relative to the season's average entrant
seasons.csvseason season, races; pi_*: probability that a result includes a routine loss; lambda_*: mean routine loss, % of race time (both at average race duration, for a dry race at a circuit of average tightness); kappa_*: pace scale κ
track.csvtrack term term (tight, mixed, wet), label; share: mean of the covariate over the races (tight score 0–1; share of mixed or wet races); pi_odds_*: factor on the odds of a routine loss; lambda_mult_*: factor on the mean routine loss (mean, q05, q95), both against a dry race at an open circuit
seasons_by_mode.csvmode × season mode (chains grouped by where the loss mixture settled; see modes.csv), then the columns of seasons.csv over that mode's draws
modes.csvchain × quantity chain, mode; quantity, label: sigma (pace noise σ, %), pi_base (routine-loss probability), lambda_base (routine-loss mean, %), rho (big-loss share of incidents), lambda_big (big-loss mean, %), from the intercepts (average race duration, before the season paths); mean, q05, q95 over that chain's draws
age_curve.csvage (years) age, rows; mean, q05, q95: population career curve, % of race time. Only its shape is identified: it has no constant or linear part

Retirement-model files

F1 1950–2025 (Jolpica API), competing-risks hazards. Effects are on the log-hazard scale: +0.5 is a rate 1.65× the average. manifest.json

FileOne row perColumns
cars.csvcar-season id, constructor, season; effect, sd: mechanical-failure log-hazard relative to the season; starts, mech_dnf: starts and mechanical retirements
drivers.csvdriver id, name; effect, sd: incident-proneness log-hazard relative to the average driver; starts, incidents, collisions
seasons.csvseason mech, incident, collision: the model's season-level rate of retirements per race distance raced, by cause (a cumulative hazard; the chance of retiring from that cause over a full distance is 1 − e−rate); *_obs: observed retirements per race distance raced

Lap-model files

F1 1996–2025, green-flag laps (Jolpica), pit laps inferred from lap times. Effects are % of the race's median clean lap; start effects are % of lap 1. manifest.json

FileOne row perColumns
drivers.csvdriver id, name, mean, sd, laps; first_season, last_season; slope_mean, slope_sd: career slope, % per decade of age, relative to the average driver; tyre_mean, tyre_sd: tyre management, % per 10 laps of stint age against the era's shared curve (negative = less degradation); starts, start_mean, start_sd: start effect, % of lap 1 against the driver's pace (negative = better starts)
cars.csvcar-season id, mean, sd, laps; dev_mean, dev_sd: in-season development, % from the season's first round to its last, relative to the field; tyre_mean, tyre_sd, starts, start_mean, start_sd: as for drivers; speed_mean, speed_sd, street_mean, street_sd, temp_mean, temp_sd: circuit slopes, % of lap time per sd of average lap speed, street score and race-time temperature, centred within the season (positive = relatively slower)
age_curve.csvage (years) age, laps; mean, sd, q05, q95: population age curve on clean laps, % of lap time. Only its shape is identified: it has no constant or linear part
modes.csvchain × quantity chain, mode, kept, quantity, label, mean, q05, q95: the scalars that split the chains into modes
vs_race_drivers.csvdriver in both models lap_mean, lap_sd: lap model; race_mean, race_sd: race model (whole career)
vs_race_cars.csvcar-season in both models lap_mean, lap_sd, race_mean, race_sd, constructor, season, laps

WRC files

WRC 2018–2025 stage times, top two tiers. Effects are % of stage time. manifest.json

FileOne row perColumns
drivers.csvdriver id (wrc.com id), name; mean, sd, q05, q95: relative to the average of all drivers in the file; stages
cars.csvmanufacturer × tier × season id, manufacturer, tier, season; mean, sd, q05, q95: within season; stage_times
