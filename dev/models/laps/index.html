Lap-time model

lap_effects / fit_laps: per-lap pace instead of whole-race times (#12). It uses partial races before a retirement and has far more data per driver–car pair. It is in development: the published fit covers 2021–2026, with the #108 and #113 terms and the per-lap track conditions (#116, #124).

Outcome and clean laps

y = 100·log(t / median clean lap of the race)

This is on the same % scale as the race-time models. prepare_laps keeps clean green-flag laps and excludes:

  • lap 1 (the standing start);
  • race-wide slow laps (safety car, VSC, red flag), detected when the field's median lap is over 1.25× the race median, plus the restart lap after each;
  • a car's laps over 2× its own median lap (a red-flag stoppage, or a very long stop), unless stoppage = false (lap_fit.jl … keepslow, #108). With recorded stops no lap is then dropped for its own size, so a car's largest incidents stay in for the big-loss component; green-flag wet laps are kept too with keep_laps (lap_fit.jl … keepwet, #116): laps with at least half the field on wet tyres where neither the lap nor the one before is neutralised (safety car, VSC or red flag, from the conditions table's neutral column; Diomedes.wet_green_laps). Over 2021–2026 they add 263 laps;
  • pit in- and out-laps, either recorded (Ergast's pit stops, 2011 on) or inferred from the lap times (infer_pit_stops).

Inferred stops give the same effects as recorded ones over 2011–2019 (correlation 0.998 for drivers, 1.000 for car-seasons). That makes 1996–2010 usable.

Model

y = γ[race] + δ[race]·(frac − mean frac) + driver + car-season + β₁[era]·s + β₂[era]·s²
    + η·lead + tyre_driver·s_d + tyre_car·s_m − (race mean) + noise
  • frac is the lap's fraction of the race distance. The per-race slope δ (partially pooled) absorbs fuel burn and track evolution.
  • s is stint age in tens of laps. With tyres, its curve (β₁, β₂) is shared within an era: the refuelling era (1994–2009) and since 2010.
  • lead = true adds η, the effect of leading the race on the lap.
  • tyre_mgmt = true adds driver and car-season slopes on stint age, centred per driver (s_d) and per car-season (s_m), as deviations from the era's curve.
  • starts = true adds lap 1 as its own term (lap_effects): its time against the race's median lap 1, with the drivers' and cars' pace effects, a quadratic in the grid slot, and driver and car-season start effects. Pit-lane starts are left out. The term has its own pace + loss noise.
  • slopes and dev add career slopes and in-season development, as in the race model.
  • age = true adds the population age curve of the race model (AgeCurveBasis): a random walk over one-year bins of season age, with its constant and linear parts projected out (they can't be separated from the career slopes and car-seasons). τ_age ~ half-N(0, 0.2).
  • circuits = true adds car-season slopes on standardised race covariates from circuit_table: average lap speed (per layout, so a wet race doesn't read as a slow circuit), street score and race-window air temperature. The slopes are centred within each season, with a spread τ_cx ~ half-N(0, 0.5) per dimension.
  • Noise is pace + loss, as in the race model. Traffic, mistakes and damage are incident losses.
  • Terms from the race model (#108): track = true puts walls, mixed and wet races on logit π and log λ (as in #81); dinc = true adds per-driver incident proneness, centred; big = true makes a share ρ of incidents big losses of their own mean (as in #16), with the proneness then on that share; rwalk = true replaces the car-season effect and the development trend with a walk of each works' chassis form from race to race (RaceWalk), pooled across off-seasons with steps for changes of ownership and major rule changes; sdrv = false drops the per-driver start effects (barely identified). With rwrel = true (lap_fit.jl … rwrel, #116) the sampler draws the walk states relative to each car's drivers, x̃ = x + W·a, with W each driver's share of a state's laps: a shear with Jacobian 1, so the model is unchanged. Over 2021–2026 it lifts the minimum ESS of drivers from 81 to 219 and of the walk from 85 to 334; rwrels also subtracts the career slopes, but moves the slow direction onto the age curve.
  • Maximum pace and consistency (#113), through a LapContext built by LapContext(d, laps; traffic, fpace, short):
    • traffic: shared effects of binned gaps from LapTraffic: to the car ahead on track (dirty air), a lapped car ahead, blue flags, a car close behind on the same lap (defending), and leading by over 5 s (cruising);
    • fpace: the field's pace on each lap (field_pace: the field's median lap, smoothed over neighbouring laps, beyond the race's mean and trend), with a slope per car-season around a shared βfp ~ N(1, 0.5). With short it also moves the shortfall's logit π and log λ (κfp), but not the edge;
    • short: driver and car-season offsets on the small-loss part's logit π and log λ, which becomes the shortfall from each driver's peak. The driver and car effects are then on the mean pace, and the clean-lap edge sits below it by the expected shortfall from the race, driver and car offsets. The field pace shapes the losses but doesn't move the edge, which would make a very stiff direction (#113: the fit then diverged on nearly every iteration).
  • Per-lap conditions (#116, 2021 on), through a LapConditions built from read_lap_conditions (the table of scripts/conditions/fetch_conditions.py: FastF1 for 2021–22, OpenF1 from 2023) and jolpica_driver_numbers:
    • cond: offsets for each compound against the SOFT (COMPOUNDS), a stint slope per compound on top of the shared curve, track temperature against the race's mean (per 10 °C) and rain falling, on the mean pace. With short also wet tyres and rain on the shortfall's logit π and log λ, left out of the edge as the field pace is;
    • wetdrv: each driver's centred deviation on intermediate and wet-tyre laps. The car-season deviations (wetdev, or wetwalk pooled through a wet walk per works) are kept for reference: in both probes they weren't identified, with 2023 cars of 30–50 wet laps from two mixed races coming out several % off;
    • wetshr: drivers' deviations as with wetdrv, plus car-season deviations for the car-seasons with at least 100 wet-tyre laps only (31 of 61 over 2021–2026), centred within season, spread ~ half-N(0, 0.5). A likelihood profile showed why the few-lap ones failed: towards a faster wet limit the losses absorb the difference, so with ~30 wet laps the likelihood is nearly flat there (#116).
    • wetall: car-season deviations for every car-season with a wet lap, centred within season; wetallg centres them over all car-seasons. Alone, either lets the few-lap car-seasons take up a race's wetness (2023's Williams at +2.6 %);
    • wetrace: a wet-lap offset per race with wet laps, spread ~ half-N(0, 2), centred over those races. It takes up how wet each race was, so with wetallg wetrace keepwet every car-season's deviation stays within ±1 % and its uncertainty grows as its wet laps fall (2021–2026 full fit, #116). Races run entirely on wet tyres stay at the prior, their offset not told apart from the race's mean lap.
    • racedeg: each race's own stint slope around the shared tyre curve, τ_rd ~ half-N(0, 1), on the stint age centred over all laps (#124). Track surface, temperature and Pirelli's nomination change how fast every compound degrades at a race, and a per-compound slope keyed to the C-number didn't separate them (the labels degrade alike once the race's level is out). racedegc puts each race's slope on the age centred within that race instead, so the slope doesn't trade off against the race's mean lap; it is the variant to use (2021–2026 full fits otherwise alike: drivers' R-hat 1.057 → 1.040, age curve 1.112 → 1.077).
    • tyreage: the stint age starts at the tyre's true age from the timing data (OpenF1/FastF1 tyre_age, less the 1 lap a new set shows), so a used set starts older. 1,574 of 6,542 stints over 2021–2026 started on used sets (median 2 laps). Extras above 10 laps are treated as unknown: they came from stops the timing data missed.
    • pitfix: reconciles Jolpica's pit stops with the timing data's tyre stints (reconcile_pit_stops, #151). A tyre change in the timing data adds a stop; a stop the timing data doesn't confirm is dropped under a safety car or red flag (277 over 2021–2026, mostly double records) and kept on green, with the timing age after it treated as unknown (53).
    • agemin=k: pools neighbouring driver-age bins, from the oldest down, until each has at least k distinct drivers, labelled by the lap-weighted mean age. With k = 3 the 27 ages 18–45 become 19 bins (18–19, 34–35, 36–37, 38–40, 41–45 merged). Ages 40–45 were Alonso's alone, and the curve there didn't converge (R-hat 1.077); pooled, it reaches 1.032.
    • agerw2: the age curve as a second-order walk, τ_age ~ half-N(0, 0.05), kept for reference. On 2021–2026 the sampler went stiff (step size 0.001–0.01 at tree depth 10): the data ask for year-to-year jumps that a smooth curve can't make, even with the tight prior.
  • Driver and car-season effects are centred as in the race model. Every term is centred within its race, so γ is the race's mean lap.

Long fits and warm starts

About 175k laps (2011–19) to 530k (1996–2025): a fit takes hours to a day on a Raspberry Pi 5. The 2011–2026 fit with every #108 and #113 term (316k laps) took about 9 h per chain at ~230 leapfrog steps per draw. Fits are checkpointed (see Running long fits). A larger fit can warm-start from a smaller one: lap_warm_start maps the adapted step size and diagonal metric across by parameter label, and lap_warm_sampler runs AdvancedHMC's NUTS from there with a shorter adaptation.

d = prepare_laps(fetch_laps(src, 2011:2019), fetch_results(src, 2011:2019); pits = :inferred)
chain = fit_laps(d; checkpoint = "output/laps_2011-2019_chain1", seed = 1000)

Known issues

  • Refuelling (1994–2009): the car lightens as fuel from its last stop burns off. A single stint curve shared across eras collapsed. The curves are now separate per era, and before 2010 the driver and car stint slopes mix tyre management with fuel strategy.
  • Two modes in the 1996–2025 fit without the age curve and circuit slopes: they differed in the spread of the per-race trends (τ_δ) and in the lap-noise mixture, with the same effects. The current fit with both terms has one mode.
  • Adapted step sizes vary between chains: in the 1996–2025 fits, some chains adapt step sizes that put them at tree depth 9 instead of 7, about four times slower. Resuming them at a fixed step size (lap_fit.jl … step=<x>) fixes this.
  • Career slopes are loose: τ_slope ≈ 2, against about 1 in the race model. Short careers at the edges of the data trade slope against level.
  • Conditions go to the shortfall: in the 2011–2026 fit the field pace moves the edge with a slope of only 0.17, and wet and mixed laps are read mostly as shortfall rather than a slower limit. The conditions terms (#116) cover 2021 on only. With them, over 2021–2026, the slope is 0.12, so they don't explain it; a 2021–2026 fit without them, to compare like for like, hasn't been run.
  • Driver and car levels mix slowly with the conditions terms: the 2021–2026 fit with cond wetdrv reached R-hat 1.08–1.09 on drivers, car-seasons and the race walk after 4 × 1,000 draws, along the ridge between a dominant driver and their car. Its conditions and wet-skill terms converged (R-hat ≤ 1.015). rwrel removes most of this: with cond wetallg wetrace keepwet rwrel the maximum R-hat is 1.019, with the age curve now the slowest (minimum ESS 188). Adding racedegc tyreage pitfix agemin=3 (#124) brings it to 1.032 (drivers, peak and age curve), with minimum ESS 141 on the age curve and about 257 leapfrog steps per draw. This is the published fit.
  • The age curve isn't identified over 2021–2026: each driver covers at most six ages and the career slopes take any steady change, so the curve's shape mixes age with who the drivers are. It isn't published, and a refit without it is planned (#159).
  • Step tuning after warm-up can overshoot: with racedeg, the step size tuned after warm-up gave deeper trees than the warm-up's own. notune keeps the warm-up's step.
