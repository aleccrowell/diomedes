Lap-time model

lap_effects / fit_laps: per-lap pace instead of whole-race times (#12). It uses partial races before a retirement and has far more data per driver–car pair. It is in development: see #12 for the current state.

Outcome and clean laps

y = 100·log(t / median clean lap of the race)

This is on the same % scale as the race-time models. prepare_laps keeps clean green-flag laps and excludes:

  • lap 1 (the standing start);
  • race-wide slow laps (safety car, VSC, red flag), detected when the field's median lap is over 1.25× the race median, plus the restart lap after each;
  • a car's laps over 2× its own median lap (a red-flag stoppage, or a very long stop);
  • pit in- and out-laps, either recorded (Ergast's pit stops, 2011 on) or inferred from the lap times (infer_pit_stops).

Inferred stops give the same effects as recorded ones over 2011–2019 (correlation 0.998 for drivers, 1.000 for car-seasons). That makes 1996–2010 usable.

Model

y = γ[race] + δ[race]·(frac − mean frac) + driver + car-season + β₁[era]·s + β₂[era]·s²
    + η·lead + tyre_driver·s_d + tyre_car·s_m − (race mean) + noise
  • frac is the lap's fraction of the race distance. The per-race slope δ (partially pooled) absorbs fuel burn and track evolution.
  • s is stint age in tens of laps. With tyres, its curve (β₁, β₂) is shared within an era: the refuelling era (1994–2009) and since 2010.
  • lead = true adds η, the effect of leading the race on the lap.
  • tyre_mgmt = true adds driver and car-season slopes on stint age, centred per driver (s_d) and per car-season (s_m), as deviations from the era's curve.
  • starts = true adds lap 1 as its own term (lap_effects): its time against the race's median lap 1, with the drivers' and cars' pace effects, a quadratic in the grid slot, and driver and car-season start effects. Pit-lane starts are left out. The term has its own pace + loss noise.
  • slopes and dev add career slopes and in-season development, as in the race model.
  • age = true adds the population age curve of the race model (AgeCurveBasis): a random walk over one-year bins of season age, with its constant and linear parts projected out (they can't be separated from the career slopes and car-seasons). τ_age ~ half-N(0, 0.2).
  • circuits = true adds car-season slopes on standardised race covariates from circuit_table: average lap speed (per layout, so a wet race doesn't read as a slow circuit), street score and race-window air temperature. The slopes are centred within each season, with a spread τ_cx ~ half-N(0, 0.5) per dimension.
  • Noise is pace + loss, as in the race model. Traffic, mistakes and damage are incident losses.
  • Driver and car-season effects are centred as in the race model. Every term is centred within its race, so γ is the race's mean lap.

Long fits and warm starts

About 175k laps (2011–19) to 410k (1996–2019): a fit takes hours to a day on a Raspberry Pi 5. Fits are checkpointed (see Running long fits). A larger fit can warm-start from a smaller one: lap_warm_start maps the adapted step size and diagonal metric across by parameter label, and lap_warm_sampler runs AdvancedHMC's NUTS from there with a shorter adaptation.

d = prepare_laps(fetch_laps(src, 2011:2019), fetch_results(src, 2011:2019); pits = :inferred)
chain = fit_laps(d; checkpoint = "output/laps_2011-2019_chain1", seed = 1000)

Known issues

  • Refuelling (1994–2009): the car lightens as fuel from its last stop burns off. A single stint curve shared across eras collapsed. The curves are now separate per era, and before 2010 the driver and car stint slopes mix tyre management with fuel strategy.
  • Two modes in the 1996–2025 fit without the age curve and circuit slopes: they differed in the spread of the per-race trends (τ_δ) and in the lap-noise mixture, with the same effects. The current fit with both terms has one mode.
  • Adapted step sizes vary between chains: in the 1996–2025 fits, some chains adapt step sizes that put them at tree depth 9 instead of 7, about four times slower. Resuming them at a fixed step size (lap_fit.jl … step=<x>) fixes this.
  • Career slopes are loose: τ_slope ≈ 2, against about 1 in the race model. Short careers at the edges of the data trade slope against level.
