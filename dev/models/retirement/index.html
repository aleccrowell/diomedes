Retirement model

retirement_effects / fit_retirement: a competing-risks survival model for how each start ends (#13). Each start is a record in laps: n laps completed, then either the race ran out (censored) or the car retired in lap n + 1 with a cause (retirement_cause):

CauseHazard hasExamples
:mecha car-season reliability effectengine, gearbox, hydraulics
:incidenta driver incident-proneness effectaccident, spun off
:collisionthe driver effect with a learned loading β_colcontact with another car
:othertreated as censoringout of fuel, driver unwell, "Retired"

Non-starters and disqualifications are dropped. RETIRE_CAUSES lists the modelled causes.

Hazards are per race distance, so short and long laps are comparable. Each cause has a constant hazard over the race plus a first-lap jump (start crashes, launch failures), both scaled by the start's linear predictor:

η_mech      = α_mech[season] + car-season reliability + race effect
η_incident  = α_inc[season]  + driver incident-proneness + race effect
η_collision = α_col[season]  + β_col · driver incident-proneness + race effect

The season levels α are random walks with Student-t(3) steps. Car effects are centred within season, race effects within season, and driver effects overall. Effects are on the log-hazard scale: a car effect of +0.5 means a mechanical failure rate 1.65× its season's average.

A retirement with no recorded cause (status "Retired", which is most of Jolpica's from 2023) counts as a retirement under the total hazard, with no split between causes (#55).

julia> prepare_retirements(fetch_results(ErgastCSV(FIXTURE), 2019))
RetireData(40 starts: 3 mech, 0 incident, 2 collision, 1 unknown cause, 34 censored; 20 drivers, 10 machine-seasons, 2 races, 1 seasons)

scripts/retire_fit.jl fits the full F1 history and writes car reliability, driver incident-proneness and per-season retirement rates.
