Findings

Results of the best race-time model so far, fitted to every classified F1 finish from 1950 to 2025, from the Jolpica API. Lapped cars enter as intervals, or at their exact gap where their own time is recorded (2023 on), and retirements from 1996 as partial gaps.

The model is the pace + loss model with race-duration and random-walk era terms for incidents, a pace scale by season, a population age curve, per-driver career slopes, big incident losses and in-season car development (see Which pace model to use). The common race level is learned (the default since #36). Earlier exports of the 1950–2019 data used the fixed race-intercept prior (_fgam), which gave the same out-of-sample fit.

Reading the numbers

  • Units. Effects are in % of race time, on a log scale. Negative is faster: a driver effect of −2 means about 2% quicker than the average driver in the data over a race distance, all else equal.
  • Drivers are in average-season units: the pace scale κ stretches or shrinks every gap in a season, and driver effects are expressed at κ = 1.
  • Car-season effects are relative to the other cars in the same season only. Comparing cars across seasons needs the drivers that link them, and isn't what these numbers measure.
  • Intervals are 90% posterior intervals.

Pages

  • Drivers: rankings with uncertainty, and every driver in one explorable chart.
  • Driver vs car: every driver-season split into the driver's part and the car's part.
  • Cars: each season's field, dominant cars, and in-season development.
  • Careers: the shape of a career, and drivers whose trajectories differed most.
  • Eras: how spread out results were, and time lost to incidents, season by season.
  • Retirements: retirement rates by cause and season, the most and least reliable cars, and incident-proneness.
  • Lap times: supporting evidence from lap-by-lap pace, 1996–2025. It checks the race model's car effects and in-season development, and isn't a second driver ranking.
  • Rallying (WRC): drivers and cars from WRC stage times, 2018–2025.
  • Data downloads: every table behind the charts, with its columns and units.
