Eras

How the sport itself changed, season by season, from the model's season-level random walks. Bands are 90% intervals.

How spread out results were

The pace scale κ multiplies every driver difference in a season. Values above 1 mean results were more spread out than average; below 1, closer. It doesn't measure how fast cars were in absolute terms.

Time lost to incidents

The probability π that a result includes a routine loss (traffic, a slow stop, a mistake), and the mean size λ of that loss in % of race time, both at average race length, for a dry race at a circuit of average tightness. Rain and walls are separate terms (see Rain and walls below), so these paths show how the sport changed apart from the weather and the circuits it visited. Rare big losses (repairs, many laps down) are a separate component and aren't shown here (see Noise: pace and incidents).

Rain and walls

How rain and walled circuits change routine losses (#81). Each race is flagged dry, mixed or wet from race reports, and each circuit has a walls score (0: mostly run-off; 2: barriers line most of the lap), both from the circuit tables. Left: how many times higher the odds of a routine loss are; right: how many times larger the mean loss is. Both are against a dry race at an open circuit, with 90% intervals; 1 means no change.

Rain is the larger effect: a wet race has about 10 times the odds of a routine loss (90% interval 5–20), and a mixed one about 3.4 times (2–6); each loss is also 16–27% larger. Walls barely change how often losses happen, but make each one about a quarter larger, as a mistake that would be a trip across the run-off costs more against a barrier. In seasons with many wet races, the dry paths above are lower than the incident rates seen in those seasons' results.
