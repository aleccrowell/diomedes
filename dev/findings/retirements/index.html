Retirements

Results of the competing-risks retirement model (see Retirements and selection bias), fitted to every F1 start from 1950 to 2025 (Jolpica API). Effects are on the log-hazard scale: +0.5 means a retirement rate 1.65× the season's average, −0.5 about 0.6×.

Retirements by season and cause

The chance that a car running a full race distance retires from each cause (lines: the model's season level; points: observed). Both are computed from retirements per race distance raced, as 1 − e<sup>−rate</sup>, so a short race or an early retirement counts for its share of a distance. Each cause is shown on its own, as if it were the only risk. Over a full race distance, a 1950s car had close to an even chance of a mechanical failure; since 2011 the chance has been under 10%. Collisions were rarely recorded as a cause before about 1990, and the season-level random walks absorb that.

Reliability

Car-season mechanical reliability relative to its season (negative = more reliable), for car-seasons with at least 20 starts: the 15 most and 15 least reliable. Intervals are ±1.645 sd.

Incident-proneness

Driver incident-proneness (solo incidents, and collisions with loading β_col ≈ 0.37), for drivers with at least 50 starts: the 15 least and 15 most incident-prone.

Slower drivers tend to be more incident-prone (correlation 0.43 with the race model's driver effects, over 149 drivers with at least 50 starts), and slower car-seasons less reliable (0.47 over 675 with at least 20 starts), as in the 1950–2019 fit (#42).
