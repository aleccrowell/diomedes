Driver vs car

The question behind the project: how much of a result was the driver, and how much the car? For each season a driver raced, the model splits their expected pace, relative to the average entrant, into two parts:

  • Driver part: the driver's pace that season: their effect plus their career terms at that age (the population age curve and their career slope), scaled by that season's pace scale κ.
  • Car part: the car-season's effect, relative to the other cars that season.

Total is their sum. All three are in % of race time for that season; negative is faster. Points are posterior means and lines 90% intervals. Type a name to see every season of a career; a driver who changed teams mid-season has a row per car.

Reading it

  • Driver and car parts are measured on the same scale within a season, so they compare directly: in Senna's Lotus seasons the driver part is about one and a half times the car part; at McLaren the car part is the larger.
  • Across seasons, the total isn't a measure of absolute speed. It's relative to that season's field, and cars are only compared within their season (see The question and what is identifiable).
  • The driver part changes over a career through κ and the career terms. Only the curvature of the age curve and each driver's slope relative to the average driver are identified, so read changes within a career, not the absolute level of one season against another driver's in another era.
