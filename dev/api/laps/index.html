API: Lap-time model

Diomedes.LapData — Type
LapData

Clean laps for lap_effects: per lap the outcome y (% of the race's median clean lap), driver, machine-season and race indices, race-distance fraction frac, stint age stint, the driver's centred season age agec (decades; 0 when the age is unknown), the race's centred season position posc and lead (1 if the car was leading at the end of the lap). With ages, abin is the lap's age bin (the driver's season age in whole years, an index into age_years; 0 when unknown) for the population age curve; with circuits, race_x holds each race's standardised circuit covariates (see prepare_laps).

Diomedes.fit_laps — Method
fit_laps(d::LapData; n_samples=1000, n_chains=1, checkpoint=nothing, seed=1, ...)

Sample lap_effects, starting near the prior centre with race intercepts and slopes from a per-race least-squares line (jittered per chain). Options as in fit_paceloss, including checkpoint (resumable blocks, one chain), and tyres, slopes, dev, lead, tyre_mgmt, starts, age and circuits as in lap_effects. With warm_start = lap_warm_start(...) (checkpointed fits), NUTS starts from that metric and step size (lap_warm_sampler) and adapts for n_adapts iterations. δ is NUTS's target acceptance rate (default 0.8). Lowering it a little enlarges the adapted step size; on large fits that can keep every chain at the same tree depth when some would otherwise adapt just past a doubling (#62).

Diomedes.infer_pit_stops — Method
infer_pit_stops(times::Vector{Float64}, skip::AbstractSet{Int}) -> Vector{Int}

In-laps of a car's pit stops inferred from its lap times (times[l] = lap l): lap l is a stop if its excess and the next lap's, over the median of the car's laps within ±5 (leaving out lap 1 and skip, the race-wide slow laps), sum to more than PIT_EXCESS_MS, the pit-lane loss. Overlapping candidates keep the largest. Against Ergast's recorded stops 2011–2019 (within ±1 lap): recall 0.986, precision 0.85; most "false" stops have a stop-sized excess (median 23 s), e.g. drive-through penalties, which Ergast does not list as stops.

Diomedes.lap_effects — Method
lap_effects(d::LapData; tyres = true, slopes = false, dev = false, lead = false, tyre_mgmt = false, starts = false,
            age = false, circuits = false)

Lap time (% of the race's median clean lap) = race intercept + race slope·frac

  • driver + car-season + tyre(stint age) + pace noise + incident loss.

  • Driver effects a_comp ~ N(0, σ_comp) and car-season effects b_mach ~ N(0, σ_mach), centred (see the model body); in the likelihood the drivers sum to zero and the cars sum to zero within each season (with race intercepts only within-season car differences are identified), as in gap_effects.

  • Race intercepts γ ~ N(μγ, 2), with μγ learned: each race's mean lap (the other terms are centred within the race, see lap_loglik). Race slopes δ ~ N(μδ, τδ): the change in lap time from the start to the end of a race (fuel burn, track evolution), partially pooled.

  • Tyres: β[1]·s + β[2]·s² with s = stint age / 10 laps, in %, one curve per era present (d.eras: refuelling 1994–2009, or not; β has 2 entries per era). With refuelling, fuel burn within a stint follows stint age too, so the curve is a within-stint pace change (tyres and fuel), kept out of the driver and car effects (#66). With tyres = false there are no tyre terms (β = 0): a check of whether they move the effects at all.

  • Career slopes (slopes = true, #15 in the race model): a linear trend per driver over their age, sl[driver]·agec with agec the lap's age minus the driver's mean age in decades, so it is orthogonal to the driver's constant effect. Over a long span of seasons a driver's pace isn't constant (Kubica's comeback, a young driver's improvement), and a constant effect then misplaces them. The slopes are centred across drivers (a common slope is not identified: the age–period–cohort problem, see CareerRows), sl = τslope·(u - mean u), u ~ N(0, 1), τslope ~ half-N(0, 0.5) in % per decade. Ages are per season (see prepare_laps), so the slopes fit only between-season change.

  • In-season car development (dev = true, #14 in the race model): a linear trend per car-season over the season, dv[machine]·posc with posc the race's position in its season (0 first round, 1 last) minus the car's mean position. A trend shared by the whole field is absorbed by the race intercepts, so the trends are centred within season: dv = τdev·(u - season mean u), u ~ N(0, 1), τdev ~ half-N(0, 1) in % per season. Without it, within-season car changes are attributed to the drivers (their slopes, #12).

  • Lead laps (lead = true, #66): η·lead, a shared effect of leading the race on that lap (in %). It can't separate clean air from pace management, which coincide; with the driver and car effects in the model it is the within-driver difference between leading and not. η ~ N(0, 1).

  • Tyre management (tyre_mgmt = true, #66): driver and car-season deviations from the shared tyre slope, td[driver]·sd + tm[machine]·sm, with the stint age s centred on the driver's (sd) and the car-season's (sm) mean, so a slope doesn't also move the level (as agec for the career slopes). Centred across drivers and within season, so they don't absorb the shared slope β[1]: td = τtd·(u - mean u), tm = τtm·(u - season mean u), u ~ N(0, 1), τ ~ half-N(0, 0.5) in % per 10 laps. Without compound data they mix tyre management with compound choice.

  • Starts (starts = true, #66): lap 1 as its own term (start_loglik), with its own race intercepts γ1 ~ N(0, 2), the drivers' and cars' pace effects plus start effects sd (drivers, centred) and sc (car-seasons, centred within season), sd = τsd·(u - mean u), sc = τsc·(u - season mean u), τ ~ half-N(0, 1), a grid-slot curve g[1]·r + g[2]·r² (r = (slot - 1)/10, g ~ N(0, 2)), and its own pace + loss noise (σ1, aπ1, aλ1). Pit-lane starts are left out.

  • Population age curve (age = true, #12; AgeCurveBasis as in the race model): a shared curve over the drivers' season ages in one-year bins, fage = proj·walk·(τage·eage), eage ~ N(0, 1), τage ~ half-N(0, 0.2) in % per year of age. Only its shape is identified: the constant goes into the driver effects and the linear part into the race intercepts and career slopes (age–period–cohort), so it is projected onto curves with neither. With it, the career slopes only explain how a driver's trajectory differs from the common one, which keeps a team's improvement from passing for both its drivers' steep slopes (McLaren 2023–25, #12). Laps of unknown age get no curve.

  • Circuit slopes (circuits = true, #14; needs prepare_laps(…; circuits)): each car-season's slope on each of the race's standardised circuit covariates (average lap speed, street score, race temperature), Σk cx[machine, k]·x[race, k]. The race intercepts take what is shared by the whole field, so the slopes are centred within season like the development trends: cx[:, k] = τcx[k]·(u - season mean u), u ~ N(0, 1), τcx ~ half-N(0, 0.5) in % per standard deviation of the covariate. A covariate that doesn't matter shrinks τcx[k] to zero.

  • Noise: pace + loss (paceloss_logpdf) with one σ, incident probability π and mean loss λ: traffic, mistakes and damage are losses, as in the race model.

Diomedes.lap_warm_sampler — Method
lap_warm_sampler(Minv, ϵ; δ = 0.8)

NUTS (AdvancedHMC, via externalsampler, uncompiled ReverseDiff) starting from diagonal inverse metric Minv and step size ϵ, with Stan's windowed adaptation of both (as Turing's NUTS).

Diomedes.lap_warm_start — Method
lap_warm_start(state, d_old::LapData, d_new::LapData; tyres_old = true, tyres_new = tyres_old,
               slopes_old = false, slopes_new = slopes_old, dev_old = false, dev_new = dev_old,
               lead_old = false, lead_new = lead_old, tyre_mgmt_old = false, tyre_mgmt_new = tyre_mgmt_old,
               starts_old = false, starts_new = starts_old, age_old = false, age_new = age_old,
               circuits_old = false, circuits_new = circuits_old) -> (; Minv, ϵ)

Diagonal inverse metric and step size for a fit to d_new, from the adapted NUTS state of a fit to d_old (only(Turing.loadstate(chain)), or its lap_adapted summary). Levels (drivers, car-seasons, races) present in both keep their adapted variance; new levels get their group's median. Scalars are scaled by the ratio of lap counts (posterior variance ∝ 1/data). Adaptation still runs on the new fit; this only gives it a good start. tyres_* and slopes_* say whether each fit has the tyre terms and career slopes (lap_effects); a block the old fit doesn't have starts from LAP_WARM_DEFAULT.

Diomedes.prepare_laps — Method
prepare_laps(laps, results; pits = :recorded, pit_stops = nothing, machine_key, circuits = nothing) -> LapData

Clean laps (see the file header) from laps (LAP_SCHEMA) with each car's machine from results (RESULT_SCHEMA). pits = :recorded takes in-laps from pit_stops (PIT_SCHEMA); pits = :inferred infers them from the lap times (infer_pit_stops). Races without lap data for their winner are skipped.

circuits (e.g. circuit_table()): one row per race with event_id, length_km, street and temp_c (and optionally circuit_id). Each race then gets three covariates (#14): average lap speed (layout length ÷ the median clean lap, km/h), the street score and the race-window air temperature, standardised across the races (race_x; raw values in race_x_raw). With circuit_id, the speed is averaged over the races on the same layout (circuit and length), so it describes the circuit rather than the day: a wet race isn't a slow circuit. Every race must be in the table.

source
Diomedes.CircuitRows — Type
CircuitRows(g::GapData, rc::RaceCircuits; train = nothing)

Per-row data for the car-season circuit slopes (#81), rows in GapData order (timed, then lapped): the machine level of each row (mach) and its race's pace covariates minus their mean over that car-season's rows (xc, rows × covariates).

As with the development trends (see DevRows), centring per car-season makes a slope orthogonal to the car-season effect: it measures how the car's form changed across circuits, and a car that raced only one kind of circuit has no slope term. train (rows used in a fit) restricts the means to those rows.

source
Diomedes.RaceCircuits — Type
RaceCircuits(g::GapData, circuits)

Race-level circuit covariates for the race model (#81), one row per race of g, from a table like circuit_table() matched by event_id:

  • x: the pace covariates of the car-season circuit slopes, standardised across the races: average speed (km/h), rough (the surface criterion S, 0–2) and race-window air temperature (°C); raw values in x_raw. Speed is the layout length × the winner's laps ÷ the winner's race time, averaged over the dry races on the same layout (circuit and length; all its races if none was dry), so it describes the circuit rather than the day: safety cars and rain don't make a circuit look slow.
  • track: the incident covariates on logit π and log λ: tight (the walls criterion W ÷ 2, 0–1) and indicators of a mixed and a wet race.

Car-season circuit slopes (% per standard deviation of each covariate), stacked by covariate ([(k - 1)·n_machines + m]): τ_cx[k]·(u - season mean u), centred within season like the development trends, since a slope shared by a season's cars is absorbed by the race intercepts.

Diomedes.circuit_table — Function
circuit_table(dir = CIRCUIT_DIR) -> DataFrame

One row per F1 race in the reference tables (1950–2025): event_id (as in fetch_results, "season-round"), season, circuit_id, length_km (the layout raced), street (the street score, 0–1), its walls and surface criteria W and S (0–2), temp_c (race-window air temperature) and condition (dry, mixed or wet). Built from race_weather.csv, layout_lengths.csv, street_scores.csv and wet_races.csv; pass it to prepare_laps(…; circuits) or the race model (RaceCircuits).

Diomedes.lap_loglik — Method
lap_loglik(γ, δ, a, b, β, sl, dv, η, td, tm, fage, cx, σ, π, λ, d::LapData)

Log-likelihood of the laps in d under pace + loss noise (paceloss_logpdf), with per-lap mean

μ = γ[race] + δ[race]·(frac - mean frac of the race) + h - (mean h of the race),
h = a[driver] + sl[driver]·agec + b[machine] + dv[machine]·posc + η[1]·lead
    + β[2e-1]·s + β[2e]·s² + td[driver]·s_d + tm[machine]·s_m
    + fage[age bin] + Σ_k cx[machine, k]·x[race, k],   s = stint/10, e = the lap's era

(stint age in tens of laps, which keeps β's two terms on similar scales; sl are the drivers' career slopes in % per decade of age and dv the cars' in-season development trends in % per season; η the lead-lap effect in %; td and tm the drivers' and cars' deviations from the shared tyre slope, in % per 10 laps of stint age, on sd and sm: s centred on the driver's and on the car-season's mean; fage the population age curve per age bin, 0 for laps of unknown age; cx the car-seasons' slopes on the race's circuit covariates d.race_x, stored column by column (covariate k of machine m at (k - 1)·nmachines + m); see `lapeffects`). As in the race-time model, centring within each race makes γ the race's mean lap, so the intercepts are not correlated with the slopes and effects: with ~1,000 laps per race the uncentred form had near-degenerate directions that kept NUTS at its maximum tree depth. Has a fused reverse rule: per-lap derivatives from ForwardDiff duals, scattered into the parameter vectors.

source
Diomedes.start_loglik — Method
start_loglik(γ1, a, b, sd, sc, g, fage, cx, σ, π, λ, d::LapData)

Log-likelihood of the lap-1 times in d (#66) under pace + loss noise, with per-start mean

μ = γ1[race] + h - (mean h of the race's starts),
h = a[driver] + b[machine] + sd[driver] + sc[machine] + g[1]·r + g[2]·r²
    + fage[age bin] + Σ_k cx[machine, k]·x[race, k],   r = (grid slot - 1)/10

a and b are the drivers' and cars' pace effects shared with the clean laps, as are the age curve fage and circuit slopes cx (see lap_loglik); sd and sc their start effects (lap 1 against their pace), and g the cost of starting further back. Centred within race as lap_loglik, with a fused reverse rule.
